# AI-assisted workflow improvement with human-controlled verification

**Turning unstructured work into repeatable, auditable delivery without outsourcing judgment**

`AI-assisted operations` · `Workflow design` · `QA planning` · `Compliance controls`

</div>

> **Outcome:** Accelerated the preparation of documentation, customer training and QA workflows by using AI for structure, task decomposition and first drafts, while keeping every technical claim tied to source evidence and every final decision under human control.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Operating context** | Recurring support, release and customer-enablement work across a portfolio of 60 production websites |
| **Tools** | ChatGPT and Claude |
| **AI contribution** | Organizing verified notes, decomposing work, drafting checklists and adapting explanations |
| **Human controls** | Sanitized inputs, source verification, manual testing, correction and final approval |
| **Business value** | Faster preparation, more repeatable workflows and clearer operational knowledge |

## 1. Situation

Supporting production systems creates a large amount of preparation work around the technical work itself: turning notes, logs, release information and customer requests into documentation, QA plans, troubleshooting guidance and training material.

The information often already existed, but it was fragmented and required substantial effort to convert into a clear, executable and reusable process. The risk was equally clear: an AI-generated answer could sound confident while being technically incomplete, unsupported or unsafe to share.

## 2. Objective

Use AI to reduce repetitive organization and drafting effort without weakening accuracy, confidentiality, traceability or personal accountability.

The goal was not to replace judgment. It was to create a faster path from **verified evidence** to a **reviewable workflow**, leaving more time for investigation, testing, customer communication and improvement.

## 3. The controlled workflow I used

| Stage | What happened | Control point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Define the outcome** | Clarified the audience, required decision and acceptable result | AI was not asked to decide an undefined problem |
| **2. Prepare the evidence** | Collected relevant notes, logs, documentation and observed behaviour | Client names, credentials and confidential details were removed |
| **3. Use AI for structure** | Used ChatGPT or Claude to organize information, decompose tasks, draft checklists or simplify explanations | Output was treated as an untrusted first draft |
| **4. Verify every claim** | Compared the draft with source documentation, logs and actual system behaviour | Unsupported assumptions were corrected or removed |
| **5. Execute and approve manually** | Performed the technical checks, testing and customer-facing review myself | AI never provided final approval or production authority |
| **6. Capture reusable knowledge** | Converted validated outputs into clearer guidance, QA steps and repeatable support material | Only verified content became part of the reusable process |

> **Operating principle:** AI can accelerate the preparation layer. Evidence, judgment, testing and accountability remain human responsibilities.

## 4. What I did

- Used ChatGPT and Claude to turn verified technical notes into clearer document structures.
- Broke complex or ambiguous requests into practical steps, dependencies, risks and review points.
- Prepared first-pass manual QA checklists from requirements and release information.
- Reworked technical explanations into customer-friendly training material for different levels of technical ability.
- Asked the tools to expose assumptions, missing information and possible failure cases rather than only produce polished prose.
- Compared every material statement with source documentation, logs and observed behaviour.
- Manually executed the checks and corrected incomplete, inaccurate or overly confident output.
- Excluded customer identities, credentials, private logs and confidential operational information.
- Reused successful structures as templates for future documentation, QA and support work.

## 6. How I determine whether an AI experiment worked

I do not treat a polished response as proof of productivity. I evaluate whether the assisted workflow improves delivery without increasing risk.

| Measure | Evidence I would compare |
| --- | --- |
| **Preparation time** | Time from verified notes to a review-ready first draft |
| **Quality** | Number of unsupported or incorrect statements found during review |
| **Completeness** | Whether critical steps, risks, dependencies and rollback actions were captured |
| **Rework** | Corrections required before the artifact could be used |
| **Reusability** | Whether the resulting checklist, guide or template could support future cases |
| **Customer impact** | Reduction in repeated explanations, confusion or avoidable follow-up questions |
| **Control effectiveness** | Zero confidential data shared and zero unchecked AI output treated as production evidence |

An experiment is successful only when it saves meaningful preparation effort **and** verified quality remains equal or improves.

## 7. Result

I produced clearer documentation, customer training material, task breakdowns and testing plans more efficiently while retaining final responsibility for every technical conclusion and customer-facing output.

More importantly, I developed a repeatable method for using AI inside operational work: automate the repetitive preparation, enforce verification and approval gates, and retain evidence of how the final result was reached.

## Recruiter takeaway

> Experiment with ChatGPT and Claude to turn verified operational evidence into reusable workflows, QA plans, troubleshooting guidance and customer training material. Measure whether the approach improves preparation time, quality and reuse; enforce confidentiality and human approval; and never treat AI output as a substitute for production evidence or accountability. This directly supports Process Street's mission to automate work, enforce standards and prove compliance.
